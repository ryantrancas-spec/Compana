ChatGPT

✔ Policy Compliant Check with school
The most widely used AI assistant in education. ChatGPT can help teachers draft lesson plans, generate quiz questions, explain concepts at different levels, summarise documents, and write feedback templates. It requires careful review of outputs before use — AI can produce plausible but inaccurate content.
Best used for
  • Drafting lesson plans and schemes of work

Things to be aware of

What Should a School AI Policy Include?

Use the template below as a guide for developing or reviewing your school's AI policy.

A
Introduction & Purpose
Set out why the school is introducing an AI policy, what AI means in an educational context, and the school's overall approach to embracing AI responsibly. Reference alignment with DfE guidance on generative AI in education.
B
Scope & Responsibilities
Define who the policy applies to — staff, pupils, governors, contractors. Name the key leads responsible for AI oversight, such as a Senior Leader, Data Protection Officer, and IT Director. Clarify how the policy links to existing safeguarding, data protection, and acceptable use policies.
C
Staff Use of AI
Outline what AI tools staff are permitted to use and for which tasks — such as lesson planning, marking, report writing, and resource creation. Emphasise that professional judgement must always remain central, and that staff are accountable for all AI-generated outputs they use.
D
Pupil Use of AI
Define acceptable AI use by pupils, including any age-appropriate tools approved by the school. Set out expectations around transparency — pupils should be clear about where AI has assisted their work. Reinforce safeguarding principles: pupils must not share personal data with AI tools.
E
Academic Integrity & Misuse
Clarify what constitutes misuse — including using AI to complete assessed work dishonestly. Reference relevant exam board guidance (e.g. JCQ) on AI use in assessments. Set out consequences for misuse and how staff will identify it.
F
Ethical Use & Bias Awareness
Acknowledge that AI outputs can reflect bias, inaccuracy, or outdated information. Require staff and pupils to critically evaluate AI-generated content. Promote fairness and inclusion in all AI-assisted activities.
G
Data Protection
Prohibit the entry of personal, sensitive, or confidential data into any generative AI tool. Require a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) before introducing new AI tools. Ensure compliance with UK GDPR and the school's existing data protection policy.
H
Cyber Security
Outline measures to ensure AI tools used are technically secure. Require evaluation of any AI tool's security features before adoption. Train staff and pupils to recognise AI-enabled threats such as deepfakes and impersonation.
I
Review & Monitoring
Commit to regular policy review — at least annually, or sooner if significant new guidance is issued by the DfE or relevant bodies. Name who is responsible for the review and how updates will be communicated to staff and pupils.
